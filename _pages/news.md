## 🎤 Two Research Presentations at IGARSS 2026

*Aug 2026 | Washington, D.C., USA*

I presented two collaborative works at **[IGARSS 2026](https://2026.ieeeigarss.org/)** in Washington, D.C.

As first author, I presented **[“Standards for AI-Enabled Earth Observation in Disaster Management: A Cycle-Based Landscape Analysis”](https://elib.dlr.de/223448/)**, together with **Nina Merkle** and **Monique Kuglitsch**. We systematically examined existing standards across the disaster-management cycle and identified where AI-enabled Earth Observation still lacks common guidance and standards.

This work is part of the **[Global Initiative on Resilience to Natural Hazards through AI Solutions](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-T/extcoop/ai4resilience/Pages/default.aspx)**, where I lead the **Roadmap Working Group**. Our work aims to connect AI-enabled Earth Observation, disaster-risk management, and standardization and to develop a practical roadmap for future work.

I also presented **[“Semi-Supervised Remote Sensing Segmentation via Foundation Model Distillation and Consistency Learning”](https://elib.dlr.de/223447/)** as co-author, together with **Pu Xiao, Xiaoyan Lu, Yuting Dong, Ji Zhao, and Yanfei Zhong**. The work explores how knowledge from foundation models can improve remote-sensing segmentation when only limited labelled data are available.

Great discussions around both methodological AI research and the broader question of how we make AI-based Earth Observation reliable and useful in real-world applications.

## 🚀 TUM Data Innovation Lab Project Selected | Pop-up City

*Jun 2026*

Very happy that my project idea **[“Pop-up City: Layered Urban Asset Reconstruction from Single Images”](https://www.mdsi.tum.de/di-lab/projekte/ws26-tum-geoinformatics-pop-up-city-layered-urban-asset-reconstruction-from-single-images/)** was selected for the **TUM Data Innovation Lab**!

The project is hosted by the **TUM Chair of Geoinformatics** in collaboration with DLR and will start in October 2026. We will explore how urban scenes can be decomposed into meaningful layers, how occluded objects and surfaces can be reconstructed, and how these representations can support urban mapping and digital twins.

Looking forward to working closely with **Thomas Fröch** and the TUM Geoinformatics team, and to seeing what the student team will build from this idea! 🏙️🚀

## 🎤 First Results of Our VLM Building Research at EGU26

*May 2026 | Vienna, Austria*

Great to present the first results from Ahmed’s Master’s research at **EGU26**: **[“Vision-Language Models for Structural Exposure Modeling from Street-Level Imagery”](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-23030.html)**, together with **Ahmed Abdelsalam, Xizhe Xue, Patrick Aravena Pelizari, and Christian Geiß**.

We investigated whether vision-language models can infer engineering-relevant building attributes directly from street-level imagery. The preliminary results show promising potential for scalable and low-cost structural exposure modelling and also highlight where domain-specific adaptation is still needed.

Great to share these early results with the community and receive useful feedback for the next stage of the work.

## 🎤 Preprint | Governing Generative AI in Disaster Risk Management

*Feb 2026*

Our perspective **[“Governing Generative AI in Disaster Risk Management”](https://eartharxiv.org/repository/view/11940/)** is now available on EarthArXiv.

The work examines both the opportunities and governance challenges of using generative and agentic AI in disaster risk management. We argue that meaningful use of these technologies in high-stakes disaster settings requires human oversight, transparency, interdisciplinary collaboration, and clear governance from design to deployment.

A timely topic as generative AI increasingly moves from experimentation toward real-world applications in disaster management.

## 🎤 New Publication | A Deep Dive into OpenStreetMap Research

*Jan 2026*

Happy to see our paper **[“A Deep Dive into OpenStreetMap Research Since its Inception (2008–2024): Contributors, Topics, and Future Trends”](https://doi.org/10.1080/13658816.2026.2613347)** published in the *International Journal of Geographical Information Science*, together with **Liqiu Meng, Andrés Camero, Stefan Auer, and Xiao Xiang Zhu**.

We analysed nearly two decades of OpenStreetMap research to understand how the scientific community, collaboration networks, and research topics have evolved, while also comparing academic research with discussions from the broader OSM community.

All data and code are openly available so that the analysis can be reproduced and extended by the community.

👉 [Data and code on GitHub](https://github.com/ya0-sun/OSMbib)
